## What this Code covers

The Family Code (Družinski zakonik, DZ) is Slovenia's central statute for family relationships and child protection. Its opening provision identifies marriage, non-marital partnerships, parent-child relationships, state assistance in partnership and family difficulties, child-protection measures, maintenance, adoption, transfer of parental care to a relative, foster care, and guardianship of children and adults needing special protection.

The Code is relevant when identifying Slovenian rules on family status, parental responsibility, child welfare and family-law proceedings. The provisions are distributed across the Code and interact with civil-status, procedural, international-private-law and other legislation. This note is a source orientation, not an exhaustive account of those provisions or a case-specific determination.

## Navigating the Code

The PISRS text is organized by parts and articles and links selected case-law references alongside provisions. Use the record's table and the relevant provision to locate the rule at issue. Broad subject areas include:

- family and child definitions, guiding principles and parental care;
- formation and effects of marriage and non-marital partnership;
- personal and property relationships between partners;
- parentage, parental care, contact and child maintenance;
- family court and administrative measures for protecting children;
- adoption, foster care and guardianship;
- maintenance between family members.

The listed subjects are an orientation, not a complete index. Confirm the actual current article text and any related amendments, court decisions or implementing rules before relying on a particular proposition.

## Scope limits and related sources

The Family Code does not itself establish all rules for registering a marriage, birth, parentage or other civil-status event. Check the Civil Status Registry Act (ZMatR) and the responsible registrar or authority for registration and documentary procedure. International cases may also require Slovenia's private international law, applicable EU instruments, treaties and recognition rules.

For a child-protection or family proceeding, identify the competent court or administrative body and the procedural statute governing that step. Where a general procedural law and a special family-law procedure interact, verify which specific provision governs the matter. The Code's broad family-law subject matter does not by itself establish an immigration residence entitlement or visa requirement.
