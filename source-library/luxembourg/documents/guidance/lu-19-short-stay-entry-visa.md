## What this source supports

The page is official procedural guidance for third-country nationals who are visa-required for Schengen entry or transit. It distinguishes airport transit visas (A) from short-stay visas (C), explains the general consular competence rules, and describes application timing, submission, supporting documents, fees as displayed, decision periods, extension, refusal notification and listed work-permit situations.

The page says applications are generally lodged at least 15 days before travel and no earlier than six months before travel (with a separate note for holders of certain multiple-entry visas). It lists identity photos, a travel document, purpose-related evidence, means of subsistence, lawful residence in the country of application and travel health insurance. It links an official financial-support process and states that such a statement is recommended, not mandatory. It also states that a visa does not itself guarantee entry or authorize paid activity.

## Scope and limits

Use this as a dated official procedural overview, not as the controlling Schengen Visa Code, Visa Handbook, nationality list, or legal advice. Confirm visa obligation and exemptions against the current official nationality list and applicable EU rules. Recheck current fees, forms, consular representation, appeal routes and work authorization before relying on them; the page's form is dated 2023 and administrative details can change. Keep this route distinct from EU-family facilitation and residence beyond 90 days.
