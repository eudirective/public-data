## Source role and accepted scope

Official AnyNet information page describing a domestic minor-adoption workflow through the national platform, accepted as the page’s undated institutional process description only.

## Review findings

The page describes one domestic minor-adoption route: an initial application online using Taxisnet or in person through local social-service bodies; spouse consent for married applicants; supporting documents; saving and editing before final submission; a submission-registration number for tracking; social investigation; preparation/training; entry in the National Register of Prospective Adoptive Parents; matching; a period of acquaintance with the child; judicial completion; and three-year post-adoption follow-up. The page states that social investigation is completed within six months from assignment to a social worker. Its embedded law table identifies Law 4538/2018 as amended by Law 4604/2019 and lists implementing material through KYA 19333/2023, so it is not a current amendment index. The final official Gazette text of Law 4704/2020, FEK A 133, 14 July 2020, Article 24 (accepted at gr-107) reissues Law 2447/1996 Article 7. Paragraphs 3–4 set a six-month period for the social-service report concerning an application for a specific child, measured from submission or service of that application regardless of requested supporting material, with a possible specially reasoned three-month extension; the six-month period is limited to domestic habitual-residence cases, while intercountry cases may receive up to two exceptional extensions. The official 13 July 2020 Parliament transcript is corroborating legislative history, not the accepted enacted source. This child-specific court-report trigger and stage differ from the AnyNet pre-matching worker-assignment workflow; the enacted clause does not validate that portal page’s timer. No form or attachment is accepted from this page review. The statutory comparison also includes Law 4538/2018 Article 26(1), as amended by Law 4611/2019 Article 92 and Law 5107/2024 Article 7: that provision includes Civil Code Article 1557 investigations and preserves a reassigned-worker cross-reference to Articles 10 and 11. The original Articles 10 and 11 address foster-care placement scenarios, so the legal application of those periods to adoption and AnyNet’s separate six-month-from-assignment statement remains unresolved.
