# Kazakhstan mission: visa guidance for family members of EU citizens

- Source ID: `pl-41`
- Page title: `Małżonkowie i dzieci obywateli państw UE` (Spouses and children of EU-country citizens)
- Publisher: Polish Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Embassy of the Republic of Poland in Kazakhstan
- Official route: https://www.gov.pl/web/kazachstan/malzonkowie-i-dzieci-obywateli-panstw-ue
- Language: Polish
- Reviewed: 29 October 2026

## Source scope

This is mission-specific operational visa guidance for family members applying through the Polish mission in Kazakhstan. The page describes a facilitated visa route when a third-country national travels to, or is already in, an EU Member State other than the state of nationality of their EU-citizen family member. It is not a nationwide visa rule, a general residence procedure, or a source for Kazakhstan residence rights. Confirm the applicant's consular district, current mission arrangements and visa category with the responsible Polish mission.

## Official indexed page summary

The official Gov.pl search result for this exact page, last crawled about a month before this review, states:

- It describes EU citizens to include EU Member State nationals, EEA-EFTA nationals (Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein) and Swiss nationals.
- It presents family categories including spouses; certain registered partners where the destination state's law treats the partnership as equivalent to marriage; dependent children under 21; and dependent parents.
- It says the application is free of consular charge and calls for a registered e-Konsulat application, two current 35 x 45 mm photographs, passport and copy, civil-status proof of the claimed family relationship, and evidence of accompanying or joining the EU citizen.
- It lists additional documents for a minor, says personal appearance and fingerprints apply to Schengen C applications but not national D applications, gives an indicative two-week processing time (up to 30 days if additional checks are needed), and says a visa does not guarantee admission at the border. It states that a C application may be submitted no earlier than six months before travel.
- It says incomplete or unregistered applications will not be accepted and the consul may request an interview.

These are the embassy page's indexed statements, not independently verified live instructions. Timelines, appointment access, documentary requirements, biometrics, fee rules and consular jurisdiction can change; check the official mission immediately before applying.

## Legal-scope caution

The page's operational description must not be treated as the definitive statutory definition of an EU citizen's family member. The current consolidated Polish EU-residence Act, Article 2(4)(a), states the core categories as spouse, qualifying direct descendants, qualifying dependent direct ancestors, and the caregiver parent of a minor EU citizen. The embassy page also refers to registered partners and dependent parents of a partner. Those references may concern a facilitation or extended-family assessment, but the page alone does not establish their legal classification or entitlement. Verify the exact visa and free-movement basis against the Act, Directive 2004/38/EC and the consulate's current instructions. EU, EEA-EFTA and Swiss legal bases must remain distinct even where the page groups them operationally.
