# Austria national-source discovery register

**Search date:** 18 October 2026  
**Intent:** discovery and preliminary rule-sustainability review; no source accepted and no retrieval adapter implemented.  
**Coverage:** bounded first pass on EEA/EU/Swiss movement, third-country family members, visa and consular routes, administrative procedure, file access, remedies, public-information access, and sample administrative-court decisions. Mission-specific instructions, systematic court-decision discovery, and land-level variants remain open search classes.

## Seed and comparison profile

The seed is Denmark's *EU-opholdsbekendtgørelsen* (BEK nr. 1457 af 06/10/2020), an official regulation specifically about residence in Denmark for people covered by EU free-movement rules. The existing Danish pack links the official Retsinformation record and PDF. Sweden's *Utlänningslag* and *Utlänningsförordning* were prior partial matches, with agency guidance as a separate source class. The previous Finland findings were provisional because direct inspection of Finlex and Migri was blocked.

Austria's selected NAG §§ 51–57 provisions are a partial functional match: the official RIS records contain an EEA/free-movement cluster within a wider settlement and residence statute, including registration and permanent-residence certificates and a Swiss extension. They are not a one-to-one counterpart to Denmark's dedicated regulation. The official Austrian portal separately explains third-country family members' residence-card route and explicitly includes Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, and Switzerland in its scope.

## Source-rule sustainability evidence

The official [RIS Open Government Data FAQ](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/UI/Ogd.aspx) says the interfaces are public and require no special registration. It also says that RIS OGD data carries a CC BY 4.0 attribution requirement, that the RIS makes no guarantee of accuracy, currency, completeness, or continuous availability, and that authentic RIS applications are legally authoritative. For larger API queries, RIS requires at most 0.5 requests per second, no parallel connections, and queries only outside office hours (20:00–05:00 Austria local time) or on weekends/Austrian holidays; it provides no bulk dumps. For a small selection of laws where daily currentness is unnecessary, the FAQ recommends obtaining the current text manually through the RIS website. This gives the monitor a no-registration, no-API starting route for a bounded source set; automated website retrieval and its change behavior still need separate runtime verification.

The preferred retrieval candidate is a small, review-only check of the public RIS current-section pages using stable locators (`Gesetzesnummer` + paragraph). The official RIS handbook documents that route, and the current § 53 page demonstrates version/date fields, but the exact raw HTML and recurring automated access behavior have not been captured. Do not pin a version-specific ELI as the only locator. A changed current section should create a review candidate, while the last accepted text remains intact. If OGD APIs are used for history/discovery, their no-registration, attribution, rate, timing, and no-bulk requirements must be enforced; they are not needed as a credentialed API. Conversion remains blocked on raw-response inspection because the research reader exposed parallel standard/expanded paragraph text.

For public guidance, pin the authority page identifier and exact heading, capture the visible page-update date and linked forms, and maintain an official topic-index/search fallback if the page moves. Hash-only alerting is not sufficient for DSB or Ombudsman HTML because navigation, forms, and page chrome can change without an operative guidance change.

## Open searches before Austria can be called broadly covered

- Mission-specific Austrian consular instructions for defined applicant locations: local appointments, checklists, representation, and family-member intake routes. The central BMEIA pages do not close this location-specific coverage.
- Broad RIS case-law discovery and update method across BVwG, VwGH, and all nine Landesverwaltungsgerichte. Four inspected decisions are examples, not a complete case-law inventory.
- Compare the NAG § 54–55 procedural postures, including the § 54(7) exception and challenges to BFA decisions. NAG §§ 3–4 give the ordinary authority/venue map, but VwGVG §§ 7–8 alone do not capture every special route or deadline.
- Information Freedom Act jurisdiction and oversight coverage across holder categories; IFG § 15 confirms a DSB advice, training, and evaluation role, but does not close the holder-specific remedy map or inventory of other oversight channels.
- Land-level practice, forms, and competent-authority variations, including Vienna MA 35 and the Tyrol/Vorarlberg ombudsman exceptions.
- NAG-DV Annex E/F current-section routes and document identities are verified; retrieve and inspect both PDF payloads, record exact bytes, and test identity and conversion before considering implementation.

This register is bounded. “Candidate found” is not proof of legal equivalence, currentness, or exhaustive national coverage.
