## Scope and use

The MAECI page is a general entry and visa FAQ. It covers visa need and applications, documentation, fees, processing times, visa validity and entries, status checks, extensions, prior consultation, refusals and remedies, the documentation listed for EU/EEA citizen family members, short-stay calculations, marriage, work, repatriation and customs. This note preserves the material relevant to visa and free-movement research; the live page remains the controlling source for its complete contents and linked services.

The FAQ’s family-member answer names family members of EU and EEA citizens and refers to Article 2 of Legislative Decree 30/2007. Do not treat the FAQ as resolving every sponsor category, the scope of Italian national law for an Italian sponsor, Swiss or UK routes, or the interaction with directly applicable EU law. Confirm the relevant legal route independently.

## Source-grounded guidance

### Visa need, application and documents

The MAECI directs users to the **Visa for Italy** database. It asks for nationality, country of registered residence, intended stay length and purpose; it then provides visa-need, document and fee information for the selected purpose. The FAQ says an application is filed with the Italian embassy or consulate responsible for the applicant’s place of registered residence. The foreign national applying is responsible for filing; an inviter does not file in their place. The database gives the responsible office’s contact details.

Applications may be submitted no more than six months before planned travel, or nine months for seafarers acting in that capacity, and generally no later than 15 calendar days before travel. The visa office may request further supporting documents beyond those listed in the database. The FAQ recommends checking with the competent office. Mission-specific instructions and current rules should be checked before applying.

### Family members of EU/EEA citizens

The FAQ says family members of EU and EEA citizens may apply under Article 2 of Legislative Decree 30/2007. It lists these documents:

1. Visa application form, accessed through the Visa for Italy database.
2. Recent photograph.
3. Valid travel document expiring at least three months after the requested visa’s expiry.
4. A declaration by the EU/EEA citizen, accompanied by that citizen’s identity document. The declaration attests to the statutory requirements, including the sponsor’s EU/EEA citizenship, residence in Italy, relationship to the applicant and, where applicable, the applicant’s status as a dependent family member; it requests the family member’s presence in Italy.
5. Civil-status records or suitable administrative evidence of the family relationship.
6. For a minor, written consent to visa issuance from the other parent.

This is the FAQ’s list, not a determination that every listed item is required in every case or that it exhausts a consulate’s current operational requirements. The FAQ itself says the visa office may request additional documents. Verify the live Visa for Italy result and the competent mission’s instructions. Read the cited statutory text and relevant EU/EEA rules for eligibility and legal effect.

### Processing and visa conditions

The page states that Schengen short-stay applications normally take 15 days, extendable to 45 days in legally specified cases (it cites Article 23 of the Visa Code). For national long-stay visas, it states a general 90-day period after an admissible application and necessary checks, with listed periods of 30 days for paid employment within the 2026–2028 entry quotas, 30 days for family reunification and 120 days for self-employment. These are the page’s published summaries; check the governing instrument and current procedure.

The FAQ distinguishes **duration** (maximum permitted stay) from **validity** (the period during which a visa may be used). It says short- and long-stay visas can allow one, two or multiple entries. For multiple entries, the permitted total stay remains bounded by the visa’s stay limit from first entry into the Schengen area. It describes six-month to five-year multiple-entry validity as exceptional and refers to Visa Code Article 24(2). The traveller should follow the dates, duration and entry count printed on the visa and applicable Schengen rules.

For status checks, contact the consular visa office where the application was filed. The FAQ says short-stay extension requests in cases of proven inability to leave or other adequately justified special circumstances are decided by the local Questura. A national visa authorizes entry; after arrival, a residence permit is required for lawful residence. Consult the Questura/State Police instructions for the relevant procedure.

### Refusals and remedies

The FAQ says submitting the requested documents does not guarantee issuance. It says reasons for refusal are notified, and the decision on a particular application is final in the sense that the application decision is not reversed; a new application or appeal may be pursued.

The page describes these appeal routes:

- Refusal of a Schengen visa, or a national visa not requested for family reasons: appeal through a lawyer to the Regional Administrative Court of Lazio (TAR Lazio) within 60 days of notification.
- Refusal of a national visa requested for family reasons: appeal through a lawyer to the competent ordinary court, with no time limit stated by the FAQ.

These are the FAQ’s general statements. For a specific refusal, read the notice, current procedural law and current official guidance, and obtain qualified legal advice promptly.

### Other summarized FAQ material

The page also directs users to the official list of nationalities subject to prior consultation; says adequate financial means are an entry condition and that a bank guarantee does not itself ensure visa issuance; summarizes the Schengen short-stay limit for visa-exempt travellers as 90 days in any 180-day period and links the European Commission calculator; says there is no dedicated marriage visa and describes a tourist-visa application with a marriage-banns certificate as advisable; summarizes quota-based work entry and the employer’s prior clearance process; and links to customs and repatriation information. These are mutable operational summaries; follow the linked official services and current law.
